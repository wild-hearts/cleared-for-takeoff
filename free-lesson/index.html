Hello, and welcome aboard. I'm Naomi, and this is Cleared For Take-Off: AI for grown-ups. If you've made it here, you have already done the hardest part. You decided to start. Everything after this is easier. By the end of this first module, Pre-Flight Checks, you'll have a real, private AI setup on your own phone, tablet or laptop, and you'll have had your first proper conversation with it. That's the whole job today. No exam, no jargon test, no keeping up with anybody else. Just a calm hour, at your own pace, with a cup of tea beside you if that helps.

Let me name the fear this module is built around, because I suspect you know it well. It goes: "I don't even know where to start, and everyone seems to already know." Two quick things. First, almost nobody around you knows as much as they are letting on; most people nodding along about AI are bluffing. Second, you are not a beginner because you are slow. You are a beginner because this technology is genuinely new. Those are very different things, and we will treat them differently the whole way through.

So let's clear the fog straight away, because most of the worry comes from not having a plain definition to hold. Here is what an AI chat tool actually is, in one sentence: it is a very well-read assistant that has read an enormous amount of writing, and is extremely good at predicting what words should come next. That is why it can talk with you, draft things for you, and explain things to you. Say it once more with me: a very well-read assistant, extremely good at predicting what words come next. That is a good enough working definition for now.

Because that is all it is, a few things follow. It is not a robot, it is not alive, and it is not secretly judging your questions. I want to underline that last one, because it is the quiet fear that stops people typing. There is no one in there rolling their eyes. You cannot ask it a silly question, you cannot annoy it by asking the same thing twice, and you cannot embarrass yourself in front of it. It is a tool that produces language. A very good one. But a tool.

Now the idea everything else hangs off. You are the Captain of this journey, not a passenger. You set the destination, you make the decisions, you keep control of the aircraft. The AI tools, which we call your crew, are here to do the heavy lifting and teach you the controls. They never take them. They read the instruments, draft the flight plan, and hand you the options, and every decision still lands with you. By the end of this course you won't be a nervous passenger hoping the technology behaves. You'll be a confident Captain who knows when to call the crew and when to fly solo.

One more thing to put down before we go on. The chat tools we'll use only reached the general public a couple of years ago. A couple of years. If you'd learned to drive two years after the motor car appeared, nobody would have called you a latecomer. So when that little voice says you've missed the boat, here is your answer, and it's worth saying out loud: you're on time, not late.

So what does today give you? Five things. You'll be able to say, in one plain sentence, what AI is and what it isn't. You'll name three things it does well and three it does badly. You'll choose your first tool, ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini or Copilot, and know why. You'll create an account safely and turn off the setting that lets it train on you. And you'll send your first message and read the reply without a knot in your stomach. That last one is the real prize, and we'll get there gently, one short video at a time.

Three myths worth putting down now, because between them they cause most of the fear. Myth one: it's science fiction. It isn't. It can't take over your bank account or watch you through the camera; it can only produce words in a chat window when you ask. Myth two: it's always right. It is not, and this may be the most important line today: it can state something completely wrong in the same warm, certain tone it uses when it's right. We have a whole module on that later, Module 5, so hold one idea for now: helpful, not infallible. Myth three: it's basically Google. It isn't. Google finds existing pages and lists them; AI writes you a fresh answer in its own words, on the spot. That is why you talk to it in sentences, not keywords, which is all of Module 2.

One quick aside for anyone who has heard the jargon. "Large language model", or "LLM", is just the engine inside these tools, the well-read word-predictor we described. "Generative AI" just means AI that produces new content rather than only sorting things. You don't need either term to use the tools, and there's a plain note on both in your workbook if you want it.

Let's land this first leg. An AI chat tool is a very well-read assistant that predicts words. It's not science fiction, it's not always right, and it's not Google. It doesn't judge you, and there's no such thing as a silly question. And above all of it: you are the Captain. In the next video we get practical, the three things this crew does brilliantly and the three it does badly, so you'll know exactly when to call on it. That's the video that makes all this useful, so I'll see you there in a moment.
