# Medaria Aid Pop-Up Store: everything to paste, and the order to do it in

Written 2 September 2026. Doable entirely from a phone. The clicking is yours; all the
writing is below, ready to copy.

The store already exists: **Medaria Aid**, `medaria-aid.printify.me`, 0 orders, 0 products,
on the `medariaaid@gmail.com` Printify account.

---

## Read these four before you start

**1. The Pop-Up Store sells in US dollars only.** Printify does not currently offer another
selling currency for this store type. UK and Australian supporters will see prices in USD.
Not fatal, and payout can still be GBP, but it is worth knowing before you price.

**2. Payouts run through Stripe, monthly.** Earnings for a month are paid on the 15th of the
following month, with no minimum. You choose the payout currency during Stripe setup, so
select GBP. Stripe holds the first payout for seven days as a standard security measure.

**3. Connect the CHARITY's Stripe, not a personal one.** Medaria Aid already has a Stripe
account. Note that this is the same account that was put under review on 3 July and had
payouts frozen until 5 August, over the Ukraine connection. If the same questions come back,
you have already written the answer once and it is in the inbox: keep that attestation handy
rather than starting from scratch.

**4. Printify pays you the profit, not the sale.** Their formula is:

> Payout = total sales − processing fee (production, shipping and taxes) − refunds

That figure, the one Printify shows you per product, is exactly the number your public
"profits fund the mission" wording is about. Do not describe the sale price as the donation.

## Step 2. Three products, not thirty

Start with three. A shop with thirty products and no sales is thirty things to photograph,
price and answer questions about. Three is enough to find out whether anyone wants any of it.

| Product | Artwork | Suggested price |
|---|---|---|
| Unisex t-shirt, Military Green and Black | `--light` | around **USD 30** |
| Tote bag, natural | `--dark` | around **USD 23** |
| Mug, 11oz white | `--dark` | around **USD 19** |

**Those prices are estimates and I have not verified them.** Printify shows the real
production cost and your profit as you move the price slider. Set the price against what it
actually shows you. A rough working rule for print on demand is about twice production cost;
below about 1.7x there is very little left after Printify's processing fee.

## Why that "where your money goes" block cannot be trimmed

It is doing three jobs, and on a Pop-Up Store the product description is the **only** place
it can do them, because the checkout belongs to Printify and cannot be edited.

1. **It defines "profit".** The Fundraising Regulator's Code requires a claim about where
   money goes to be accurate and substantiated. "Profits fund the mission" needs profit
   defined, and Printify's own payout formula is what defines it here.
2. **It says this is not a Gift Aid donation.** Medaria has a live Gift Aid declaration form
   and a `giftaid@` alias, so a supporter assuming a purchase qualifies is a real risk, and
   so is Gift Aid being claimed on trading income.
3. **It tells the buyer what they are paying for before they pay**, which is consumer law.

The claims from the original merch document, "buying one shirt = 1/3 of a trauma kit",
"5 patches = 1 full med pack", are deliberately **not** here. Each needs a costing you can
produce if the regulator asks. If you have that costing, put them back. If not, leave them out.

## Step 4. Before you tell anyone

- **Order one item yourself.** It exercises payment, production, shipping and the emails, and
  it shows you what a buyer actually receives. Refund yourself afterwards.
- **Check the print on the real garment.** Colour on a screen is not colour on cotton, and
  you are asking people to wear this on behalf of a charity.
- **Add the store link to medariaaid.com** and to the social accounts.
- **Consider a custom domain.** Printify supports pointing one at a Pop-Up Store, so
  `merch.medariaaid.com` reads better than `medaria-aid.printify.me` and keeps the option to
  move the store later without breaking the link.

## Still an accountant's question, not mine

Selling branded merchandise is generally non-primary-purpose trading for a UK charity. Above
the small trading exemption it becomes taxable, and the usual answer is a trading subsidiary
that gift-aids its profits up to the charity. Confirm the current threshold before volume
matters. Also tell them the income will arrive as a monthly Stripe payout from Printify
rather than as direct sales, so it is not a surprise at year end.
